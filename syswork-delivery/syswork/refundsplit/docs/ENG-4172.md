# ENG-4172 — Refund totals don't reconcile

**Reported by** Finance Operations
**Severity** High — affects the monthly reconciliation, not a single customer

## What we're seeing

Our refund ledger is short against the payment processor almost every day. The
gap is small and it is always in the same direction: we record slightly *less*
refunded than the processor says we refunded.

August: processor says **$1,284,406.12** refunded, our ledger says
**$1,284,002.51**. That is **$403.61** across 40,361 refunds — almost exactly
one cent per refund.

## The case we can reproduce

An order with three lines of $10.00 each, fully refunded for $100.00 of
merchandise value:

| Line  | Line total | Refund share recorded |
|-------|-----------:|----------------------:|
| L-001 |     $10.00 |                $33.33 |
| L-002 |     $10.00 |                $33.33 |
| L-003 |     $10.00 |                $33.33 |
|       |            | **$99.99** — one cent short |

The customer's card is refunded $100.00. Our books record $99.99. The penny has
nowhere to live, so reconciliation flags the order.

## What makes it hard to spot

Most refunds divide cleanly. An order of $50 / $30 / $20 refunded in full
allocates exactly, sums exactly, and looks perfect. The break only appears when
the division leaves a remainder — which, across a real order book, is most of
them, but never the one you check by hand.

## What "fixed" means

The per-line shares must sum to the refund total. Exactly. For every input,
not just the ones in the test file.

## Out of scope

Tax and shipping allocation, multi-currency, and partial refunds spanning more
than one payment method. This ticket is the arithmetic only.
